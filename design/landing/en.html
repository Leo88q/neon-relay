A potato arena where
burn-out decides

A neon room where two teams of potatoes share one hall. Burn-out (NeonDM): play to a score limit, sudden death on a tie, and the only weapons in play are the ones that actually exist in the mode — pistol at spawn, shotgun, grenades and laser off the floor. Everything else is cosmetics and your own local progression.

the mode

What burn-out is

round

Score or clock

A score limit and a minute limit, both server settings. Exactly one player has to break into the lead, otherwise the round goes to sudden death.

survival

10 HP + 10 armor

Armor does not absorb everything: any hit above 1 damage leaks one point through. Respawn is 500 ms, so no long pauses.

drops

Pickups respawn after 15 s

The controller lets you pick up shotgun, grenade and laser only. No ninja, no armor or health packs — on purpose.

scoring

A hammer kill scores nothing

A hammer hit counts as an administrative action: knockback yes, point no. Suicides cost a point.

the armoury room

Six weapons, laid out in their niches

The room is the one the client draws: data/ui/backgrounds/arena_armory.png. The cards come from a single atlas, data/ui/arsenal/cards_6.png, and the HUD icons are cut from the same sprites the weapons use in combat — so "the pretty picture" and "the thing flying at you" are the same object.

features

Ten reasons people play this

Every line is something the client and the server actually do — not a roadmap. The numbers are pinned by a test: scripts/test_landing_pages.py checks them against the catalogues.

physics

A hook that decides fights

Movement is the engine's own physics: hook, boost, grenade bounce. Your own blast pushes you exactly like an enemy one, which is how people gain speed and reach ledges.

the mode

Burn-out to a limit

Score limit or time limit; if the lead is shared, the round goes to sudden death. 10 HP and 10 armor, 500 ms respawn, no long pauses.

arsenal

6 weapons = 6 icons

The HUD icon is the same sprite the weapon uses in combat, and the armoury card is cut from one atlas. Menu and game cannot disagree about how a gun looks.

drops

Pickups every 15 s

The controller only lets shotgun, grenade and laser into the world: loot is predictable, and nothing buffs you out of nowhere.

maps

27 maps, each with a blueprint

Every preview is a diagram of that map's own data: rock, death, no-hook, checkpoints, spawns. You can see what a map punishes before you join it.

skins

10 potatoes

A character catalogue with rarity and lore; skills are story only, with no bonus to physics or damage. Try-on is free and needs no wallet.

progress

3 quests a day

Levels up to 30, a day streak, three quests from a pool of 12 and XP for practice, try-ons and joining a server. All of it local, in the client config.

wallet

Optional by design

Warm-up, races, try-on and progression work without a wallet. The wallet only shows what already belongs to you; there are no purchases in the client.

craft

One style everywhere

Nine backdrops of one room, one icon set and one colour table (neon_style.h) — from the Play button to the finish banner.

honesty

Zero earning promises

No "earn while you play", paid race entry stays off until the server side is configured, and "verified" is stamped by a match signature, not by the client.

progress and money

Local progression, said honestly

there

What you level up

  • Level — curve 250·L·(L+1)/2, capped at 30.
  • Day streak — counted in UTC days, no "magic" reset.
  • 3 daily quests from a pool of 12 — warmup, trying on a character, joining a server.
  • XP sinks: starting practice 25, stopping 40, trying on 10, session 15.

Progress lives in the client config (cl_neonrelay_*) and is labelled local on purpose: it does not turn into money and does not move between accounts.

not there

What this game will not promise

  • No "earn while you play", no payout promises of any kind.
  • Paid race entry is unavailable: the button exists, but it takes no money until the server side is configured.
  • NFT skin purchases are switched off in the client; the storefront only shows what you already own.
  • "Verified" is not stamped by the client: a match signature confirms the result, and the leaderboard shows only what passed that check.
how it is drawn

One room, one interface language

tokens

Colors are not eyeballed

The night palette, radii 16/12, the 2 px border and the dimming value live in src/game/client/neon_style.h, and menu and HUD code reads them from there — 231 stray color literals were removed from the code.

rasters

Not one letter baked in

Backdrops and cards are generated without pseudo-text; the client's font draws letters. Characters are never painted into backgrounds either — the portrait is drawn on top, so it cannot fight the frame.

CI

Assets are reproducible

The bake for backdrops, atlases and blueprints is deterministic: --check rebuilds everything into a temp directory and compares byte for byte. A separate test guards that the sprite grid matches the pixel pitch of each sheet.

faq

The confusing parts, briefly

Do I need a wallet to play?

No. Warmup, local races, trying on characters and all progression work without a wallet. The wallet is only there to prove that a skin you bought earlier really belongs to you.

Why show the ninja if it never drops?

So an empty niche does not look like a bug. The page labels the weapon "not in the mode" exactly like the HUD dims an unavailable cell.

Did you really remove emoji from chat?

Yes. The friend heart and the spectator-list marks are sprites from data/gui_icons.png (a 12×3 grid of 32 px cells), not glyphs: they do not need a codepoint in the font, and their color comes from a setting. The map star rating stayed a glyph — a test parses that string, and the scoreboard has no sprites to draw.

Is there a shrinking zone, like a battle royale?

No. Burn-out is a duel to points with a time limit and sudden death: no closing circle, no 60 potatoes on one map. This page does not promise what the code does not do.
